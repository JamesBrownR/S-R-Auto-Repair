Your suspension system does more than just make the ride comfortable — it keeps your tires in contact with the road and your car in control. When it wears out, you'll feel it. Here's what to watch for.

## 1. Clunking or Knocking Over Bumps

A clunking noise when you hit a bump or pothole is one of the most common signs of worn suspension components. It usually points to worn ball joints, tie rod ends, sway bar end links, or strut mounts. These are metal-on-metal connections with rubber bushings that absorb shock — when the rubber wears out, metal hits metal and you hear it.

Don't ignore clunking. These components affect your steering and handling, and they don't get better on their own.

## 2. The Car Pulls to One Side

If your car drifts left or right without you steering that way, the culprit could be worn suspension components, but it could also be alignment, tire pressure, or brake issues. The important thing is to get it checked — pulling is a handling problem that makes the car harder to control and accelerates tire wear.

## 3. Excessive Bouncing After Bumps

Try the bounce test: push down hard on the hood or trunk of your car and let go. It should settle after one or two bounces. If it keeps bouncing, your shocks or struts are worn out and no longer controlling the spring rebound. Bad shocks mean longer stopping distances and reduced handling in emergency situations.

## 4. Uneven Tire Wear

Your tires can tell you a lot about your suspension. If you're seeing cupping (scalloped dips around the tread), feathering, or wear that's uneven from side to side, worn suspension components are often the cause. An alignment check and suspension inspection together will find the problem.

## 5. Nose Diving When Braking or Body Roll in Corners

If the front of the car dips sharply when you brake, or the car leans noticeably when cornering, your shocks and struts are doing a poor job of controlling the vehicle's weight transfer. This affects both comfort and safety — especially in emergency maneuvers.

## Common Suspension Components and How Long They Last

- **Shocks & struts:** 50,000–100,000 miles (varies greatly by driving conditions)
- **Ball joints:** 70,000–150,000 miles
- **Tie rod ends:** 70,000–150,000 miles
- **Sway bar end links:** 50,000–100,000 miles
- **Control arm bushings:** 100,000+ miles, but Florida heat accelerates rubber degradation

Florida roads and heat are harder on rubber bushings than most places. If you've got high mileage and haven't had a suspension inspection, it's worth having it looked at.
