Your brakes are the most important safety system on your vehicle. Yet most drivers ignore warning signs until a small fix turns into a big, expensive repair. Here's what to watch for — and why acting early always saves you money.

## The Most Common Warning Signs

**Squeaking or squealing when you brake**
Most brake pads have a small metal wear indicator built into them. When the pad gets thin, that indicator scrapes against the rotor and makes a high-pitched squeal. This is intentional — it's the brake pad telling you it's time. Don't ignore it.

**Grinding noise**
If you've gone past squealing and you're now hearing a grinding metal-on-metal sound, your pads are worn completely through. Now you're damaging the rotors too. What would have been a $150–$200 pad replacement can turn into a $400–$600 rotor-and-pad job very quickly.

**The pedal feels soft or goes to the floor**
A spongy brake pedal usually points to air in the brake lines or a problem with the master cylinder. Either way, this is a safety issue — don't drive the car until it's inspected.

**The car pulls to one side when braking**
If your vehicle pulls left or right when you apply the brakes, one caliper may be sticking or one side of pads may be more worn than the other. This affects your ability to stop straight and should be addressed promptly.

**Vibration or pulsing when you brake**
A pulsing or shuddering brake pedal usually means your rotors are warped — often from overheating. You'll feel it through the pedal or steering wheel when slowing down.

**The brake warning light is on**
Some vehicles have a brake pad wear sensor that triggers a dashboard light. Others use this light to indicate low brake fluid. Either way, it's worth getting checked.

## How Long Do Brakes Last?

It depends entirely on your driving habits and vehicle. City driving with lots of stop-and-go is much harder on brakes than highway miles. As a rough guide:

- **Brake pads:** 25,000–65,000 miles
- **Rotors:** 50,000–70,000 miles
- **Brake fluid:** Every 2 years or 30,000 miles (often overlooked)

If you're not sure when yours were last replaced, a free visual inspection takes about 10 minutes.

## What Happens If You Ignore Bad Brakes?

The longer you wait, the more expensive it gets — and eventually it becomes dangerous. Worn pads damage rotors. Damaged rotors can affect calipers. What started as a simple pad swap can cascade into a full brake system repair.

More importantly, compromised brakes mean longer stopping distances. In an emergency situation, that difference can matter a lot.
