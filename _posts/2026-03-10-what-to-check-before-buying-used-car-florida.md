Buying a used car is one of the biggest financial decisions most people make. In Florida, there are a few extra things to watch out for that you won't find in most generic buyer's guides. Here's what to look for — and why having a mechanic inspect the car before you buy is almost always worth the cost.

## Florida-Specific Things to Watch For

**Rust — even in Florida**
People assume Florida cars don't rust because there's no road salt. That's mostly true for the undercarriage, but Florida's humidity and coastal air create a different kind of corrosion. Check around the wheel wells, rocker panels, and underneath for rust that's been painted over or hidden with undercoating.

**A/C system condition**
In Florida, the A/C runs almost year-round. That means more wear on the compressor, condenser, and hoses than in most states. Ask when the A/C was last serviced, and make sure it blows cold quickly. A weak A/C on a Florida car is a sign of deferred maintenance or a system that's near the end of its life.

**Flood damage**
Florida floods. Vehicles can be totaled in a flood, repaired cosmetically, and resold with a clean title in some cases. Look for waterlines in the trunk, under the seats, or in the engine bay. Check for musty or mildew smells — even after cleaning, flood damage often leaves a distinct odor. Look for corrosion on electrical connectors and underneath the dash.

**Overheating history**
Florida heat is hard on cooling systems. Look for milky or frothy residue on the oil cap (a sign of coolant mixing with oil, which can indicate a blown head gasket). Check the coolant for oil contamination. Ask the seller if the car has ever overheated.

## General Used Car Checks

**Check engine light and fault codes**
Even if the dash looks clean, have the codes pulled at a shop. Some sellers clear codes before selling. If you drive the car for 15-20 minutes and have the codes pulled, any pending codes will often reappear.

**Tire wear patterns**
Uneven wear tells you about alignment, suspension condition, and how the car was driven. Cupping suggests bad shocks. Inside edge wear suggests alignment or suspension problems.

**Test all the electronics**
Windows, locks, mirrors, heated seats, infotainment — test everything. Electrical repairs on used vehicles can be expensive and frustrating.

**Check the transmission**
Drive it through all the gears. It should shift smoothly without hesitation, slipping, or clunking. On an automatic, it should engage smoothly from Park to Drive with no delay.

**Look under the car**
Check for fresh oil spots, leaking fluids, damaged exhaust components, and worn CV axle boots. A quick look from a low angle at each corner will tell you a lot.

**Pull the vehicle history (Carfax or similar)**
Not a substitute for a physical inspection, but useful for flagging accidents, title issues, and maintenance records.
